Guides & recipes

Different ways crews actually use Atmosphere — the situation, what to hand the agent, and what comes back.

Sales

Revive the pipeline on a Friday afternoon

Ask the agent for every lead with no movement in ten days. It comes back with the list, what is owed to whom, and a drafted follow-up for each — you approve, it sends nothing until you do.

Walk into a callback knowing everything

Picking up a colleague's lead cold? Ask for a summary of the lead's history. You get the property, the loss, every conversation so far, and the open question the customer is actually waiting on.

Operations

The estimate before the morning meeting

Yesterday's scan and photos are already attached to the job. Hand the Mitigation Estimator the job and by the time coffee is poured there is a priced, S500-classified Xactimate estimate — with the unbilled-work check already run.

Let the morning brief run the standup

The Operations Platform's rules watch every open job overnight — overdue readings, stalled drying, idle equipment, unstaffed starts. The morning brief lists what actually needs a decision, so standup is ten minutes, not forty.

Demobilize the day the job dries

When every area hits its dry standard, the platform says so. Sign off, pull equipment the same day, and stop losing billable units to jobs that finished last Thursday.

Roofing

Storm week, kept straight

The night after hail, leads arrive faster than anyone can work them. Every inspection lands as a lead with its photos and measurements attached; the agent drafts the carrier-ready estimate from what was captured, and flags which claims are still waiting on an adjuster — so nothing melts away in the pile.

Field

The two-minute walk-through

Start recording at the door. Call out readings, let the camera name the materials and damage, speak the notes you would otherwise forget. Everything lands on the job as evidence — and tomorrow's estimate reads it.

Manager

Catch the margin while the job is open

Job costing runs against the live job: labor, equipment, materials, and subs set against what the estimate said. A job crossing its margin flags while there is still something to be done about it.

Month-end in an afternoon

Invoices build from the record — estimate, change orders, work performed — and will not go out with a blocking document missing. The margin breakdown by carrier, crew, and job type tells you which work to chase next month.

Everywhere

Answer a dispute with a replay

A carrier questions a line item six weeks later. Open the audit trail, replay the run that priced it, and send what the agent saw, what it did, and what the Verifier confirmed.
