The problem

Everything was said out loud, and nothing survived it

"Who told you to demo that wall." "The super said it was fine." "I was working off the old scope." The money in this trade is lost in a narrow set of places that are all the same failure underneath: two companies working from different facts, and no record of either. Photos are curated. Memories argue. Pay applications run on trust.

The disputed Tuesday

A GC and a sub in front of a mediator, arguing about one day of work eight months ago. Whoever kept the better record wins — and neither kept one.

The unpayable pay app

"Is the work actually done" costs a site visit or an act of faith. Most weeks it gets the act of faith, and some weeks that is expensive.

The eighteen-month read-back

An adjuster, an attorney, a carrier auditor — somebody eventually reads the job back. What they are handed today is a photo folder and an invoice.

How it works

Four stages, every day, on every job

The crew presses one button in the morning and holds it at the end of the day. Everything else happens to the footage, not to the crew.

01

One button, all day

Tap it when you get to work, hold it when you are done. One recording covers the whole day and every job in it. Nobody picks a job, tags a video, or types what they filmed.

02

Checked at the door

GPS puts the phone on the site. The clock puts it on the day. The hash proves it was never re-cut. A clip filmed two miles away is flagged before anybody watches it.

03

Read by the assistant

AI reads the footage against the agreed scope, watches the stage of work live while filming, and writes a narrated report — what was done, what was covered, what it could not see.

04

Held on the record

Sealed, labeled, and entered into an append-only chain of custody. Every view, share and download afterward is an entry under a verified name.

Capture

The scope arrives as a document

Upload the signed work order or the carrier estimate the job already has. The assistant reads it into proposed scope lines — including the exclusions — and a person keeps, prunes and confirms them. Nothing becomes scope until somebody says so, and every line it creates carries that document as its source. When the argument comes eighteen months later, the line points back at the page it came from.

InputSigned work order or carrier estimate, PDF
ExtractionA proposal — never the scope by itself
ProvenanceEvery line stamped with its source document
UnreadableShown to you, never quietly dropped
Verify

Integrity first, meaning second

Before the footage is read for content, it is checked as evidence: filmed on site, filmed on the day claimed, never filed before, never altered since. The checks are stored as findings from the moment of upload — what was known when somebody decided, not what today's code would conclude. Only then does the AI read what the work actually shows.

LocationGPS vs. the property, in feet
TimeCapture vs. upload, in minutes
FingerprintSHA-256, sealed at arrival

Report

A seven-hour day, read hour by hour

A workday does not fit in one look, so it is not read in one. The recording is cut into time windows, every window is read against the agreed scope, and one pass writes the day from those readings. What comes back is a verdict per scope line with the hours it was seen being worked — "read in 20 of 21 time windows" — plus the day told in order, so a project manager can find the hour something happened instead of scrubbing footage.

LengthWhole workday, 7+ hours
MethodTime windows, read one at a time
CoverageCounted from windows, never asserted
VerdictsCapped by how often a line was seen
Attribution

The day sorts itself into jobs

One recording, several jobs. Which stretch of footage belongs to which job is decided from where the phone was and when — not from anything a crew types at the end of a long day. A stretch that cannot be placed is handed to a person with the reason attached, never filed to whichever job happened to be nearest. Guessing would be the one dishonest thing this whole product exists to prevent.

The rules

Three rules that make it evidence, not footage

A verification platform is only worth what it refuses to say. These three refusals are structural — enforced by the database and the screens, not by policy.

unknown ≠ pass

Unchecked is not clean

A clip with no location reads as unverified, styled as neither pass nor fail. "We could not tell" is an answer the system is required to be able to give.

AI ≠ acceptance

A reading is not a sign-off

The model's reading and a person's acceptance are different claims, rendered differently everywhere. Money moves on the second, never silently on the first.

append-only

The log outranks its owner

Nothing in the chain of custody can be edited or removed — including by whoever owns the job. A custody record the custodian can change is not a custody record.

Sharing

Hand the record to the person who releases the money

Adjusters, examiners and attorneys review evidence in the Verifier — a records screen, not an operations console. Shares are account-to-account: issued to a named person's email, opening only for an Atmosphere account signed in with it. Forwarded, the link refuses — which is what puts a verified name on every custody entry instead of "someone with the link".

Watching is free

The reviewer sees every clip on the job, the checks, the analysis and the custody trail. Every view lands in the log under their name.

Keeping a copy is the paid act

A download settles your organization's fee first, through an append-once ledger. No signed file leaves until the ledger reads paid or waived.

Revocation is immediate

A share dies the moment you revoke it, and both the share and the revocation are custody entries. Who could see what, and when, is always a query.

Subcontractors

Your subs will not keep an account with you

A drywall crew works for six general contractors. Asking them to sign up before they can read the scope is how a rollout dies in the first week — so a sub opens a link and starts working, with no account, ever. The token in the link is the whole credential.

One screen, every contractor

A sub who verifies a phone or an inbox once gets every job they have been invited to — across every GC — on a single screen. Today's work first, because at 7am that is the only question.

You see your own invitations, and nothing else

You can see which of your links a sub actually opened — the audit answer to "did they ever see the scope". That the same crew also works for your competitor is not yours, and nothing here shows it to you.

Identity is proven, never guessed

Two contractors both typing "Mike's Drywall" is not evidence they mean the same business. Only a code sent to a phone or an inbox joins jobs together, so nobody is merged by a coincidence of spelling.

Who it's for

Both sides of every check that changes hands

Verification is not surveillance of one side by the other. The sub's proof and the GC's certainty are the same file.

General contractor

Pay on footage, not on faith

"Can I release Tuesday's draw" is answered by Tuesday's clips, read against the scope, before the crew has left the site.

Subcontractor

The day you worked is provable forever

One button a day, and no more arguing about what state the site was in when you arrived — or what you never touched. Every GC who invites you shows up on one screen.

Adjuster & carrier

Settle from the record

Every clip, its integrity verdicts and its narrated report, on one screen — with the chain of custody that makes it citable.

Attorney & mediator

Read the job back

Eighteen months later, the disputed Tuesday is footage with a hash, a location, a reading and every access since — not two memories.
