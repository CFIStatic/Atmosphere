Operations Platform

Run the work

A project manager runs fifteen to forty jobs at once, and the one that goes wrong is never the one they are looking at. The Operations Platform watches all of them — the readings, the paperwork, the equipment, the crew — and writes the estimates.

PROJECT MANAGEMENT ESTIMATING ASSISTANCE
Project management

The agent is not smarter than your PM. It is just never bored.

Nineteen deterministic rules run over every active job, continuously. The model never decides what is true — the engine computes the facts first, and only then does anything get written.

Drying

The dry-out, watched daily

A reading nobody took on Tuesday puts a hole in Wednesday's log, and a carrier can knock the whole dry-out for it. The rules catch the missed reading, the moisture that stopped falling, the area that is under-equipped for what is recorded wet — and the job that is already dry and should demobilise.

CatchesOverdue readings · missed monitoring visits
DetectsStalled drying · under-equipping vs S500 sizing
CallsDry standard met — sign off and pull equipment
Paperwork

Requirements, dated from the loss

A new job arrives carrying its own documentation checklist — the requirements for this trade, this loss type, and whether it is an insurance claim — with commitments counted from the loss date, because that is what a carrier counts from.

BuildsThe checklist for this job, on creation
CountsFrom the loss date, not from intake
BlocksInvoicing when a required document is missing
Equipment & crew

The dehumidifier nobody went back for

Equipment sitting on a job that dried out last Thursday is not billable there and not on the next job either. The same rules watch crew load, unstaffed start dates, and jobs with no project manager on them at all.

FindsIdle equipment · overloaded crew · unstaffed starts
NoticesStale jobs · no next step · target date at risk
WritesA morning brief, from computed facts only
Estimating

Estimates written from the evidence

Both estimators start from what was actually captured on site, and both end in Xactimate — priced, documented, and defensible. That is as true for a storm-hit roof as for a flooded kitchen.

Mitigation

Mitigation Estimator

Give it the DocuSketch scan, the MICA report, the iPhone photos, and the field notes. It classifies the loss against IICRC S500, writes to the carrier's program terms, and prices every line.

Then it does the thing estimators rarely have time for: it reads the record again looking for work that was performed but never billed.

ReadsDocuSketch scan · MICA report · photos · field notes
WritesPriced, documented Xactimate estimate
ChecksS500 classification · program terms · unbilled work
Construction

Construction Estimator

The rebuild estimate starts where mitigation ended. The agent signs in to DocuSketch itself, reads the scan and the photos, finds the matching job in your CRM, and reads the mitigation estimate that came before it.

Signs inDocuSketch
ReadsScan · photos · matching job · mitigation estimate
WritesConstruction / rebuild estimate for Xactimate
