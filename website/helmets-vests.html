The mounts

Two places to put a phone on a person

Both hold the phone the crew already carries — no second camera to charge, sync, or lose. Same app, same footage, same chain of custody. The only question is whether the work is in front of the eyes or in front of the chest.

Helmet

On the hard hat, looking where you look

The mount clamps to the brim of a standard hard hat or bump cap and holds the phone at eye level. The footage follows the head — into the panel, under the sink, up the ladder — which is exactly the angle a reviewer needs when the question is what the tech could see.

Mounts toHard hat brim · bump cap
HoldsThe phone, cased or bare
AngleTilts to the work, then locks
Best forTight spaces · overhead work
Vest

On the chest, steady through the walk

The vest is a harness worn over the shirt or jacket with the phone held flat against the chest. It sits lower and moves less than a head does, so the before-and-after clips come back steady and level — the walk-through a customer can actually follow.

WornOver shirt or jacket
HoldsThe phone, flat to the chest
AdjustsStraps at shoulder and waist
Best forWalk-throughs · long jobs
Why mounted

A mount is not an accessory

Every clip that fails the door check fails for a reason someone could have avoided. A phone that stays put removes three of them.

01

Nobody stops working to film

The most expensive part of documentation is the pause. Mounted, the recording runs through the job instead of between the parts of it, and the shot list fills itself as the tech moves.

02

The angle is the tech's angle

Held at arm's length, a phone shows the room. Worn, it shows the work — close, lit by the same headlamp, from the position the hands are working in.

03

Steadier footage clears the door

Field Capture checks each clip for location and time before it counts. Footage that isn't swinging from a wrist is easier to read against the scope — and easier for a customer to follow eighteen months later.
