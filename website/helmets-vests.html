How it works

Four steps, and none of them is "hold the phone"

The mount is not a camera. It is a bracket that holds the phone the crew already carries, so the app that was always going to film the job can do it without a hand. Everything downstream — the door checks, the AI read, the chain of custody — is the same pipeline as any other clip.

01

Clip the phone in

The phone goes in the cradle — on the brim of the hard hat, or flat against the chest on the vest. No second camera to charge, sync, or lose, and nothing new for the tech to learn to operate.

02

Open the job, hit record

Field Capture pulls the shot list from the job's own scope. One tap starts the recording; from there the tech is looking at the work, not at a screen.

03

Work with both hands

The recording runs through the job instead of between the parts of it. The camera names what it sees, the assistant answers out loud, and the shot list fills itself as the tech moves through the site.

04

Checked at the door, held

The footage lands in the Evidence Platform like any other clip — placed by GPS, dated by the clock, read against the agreed scope, and held in a chain of custody the customer can open later.

The two mounts

Head height or chest height

Same phone, same app, same record. The only question is whether the work happens in front of the eyes or in front of the chest — and most crews end up owning both.

Helmet

On the hard hat, looking where you look

The mount clamps to the brim of a standard hard hat or bump cap and holds the phone at eye level. The footage follows the head — into the panel, under the sink, up the ladder — which is exactly the angle a reviewer needs when the question is what the tech could see.

Vest

On the chest, steady through the walk

The vest is a harness worn over the shirt or jacket with the phone held flat against the chest. It sits lower and moves less than a head does, so the before-and-after clips come back steady and level — the walk-through a customer can actually follow.
